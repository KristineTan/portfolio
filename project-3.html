T127 / Capstone Product

Introducing AI Tools That Create Productive Struggle: Session 3 of the LEAP E-Module

Our capstone is a four-session Canvas course designed to help faculty understand and harness productive struggle in AI-assisted learning. My contribution is Session 3 — the pivot point where conceptual groundwork becomes practical action: introducing the specific AI tools that can generate productive struggle and equipping faculty to experiment with them confidently.

The Arc of the Full Course

The four sessions follow a deliberate sequence. Session 1 establishes shared language by defining productive struggle and why it matters in learning. Session 2 grounds that definition in research, examining what we know about AI's role in creating or undermining it. Session 3 — my session — moves from understanding to action, introducing tools that can actively generate productive struggle in a faculty member's own teaching context. Session 4 closes the loop by encouraging faculty to experiment openly with these tools and develop their own stance toward AI in education.

This arc matters because productive struggle is an easy concept to endorse and a difficult one to enact. The course is designed so that by the time faculty reach Session 3, they are not encountering tools in the abstract — they already have a framework for why these tools are worth using and what good use looks like.

My Role: Session 3

Session 3 is where the course shifts from "what is this?" to "what do I do with it?" My design task was to introduce a curated set of AI tools without overwhelming faculty, while making clear how each tool connects to the productive struggle principles established in the earlier sessions.

The analysis accompanying this deliverable documents the decisions behind that curation: why I selected certain tools over others, how I categorized them by the type of cognitive challenge they support, how I explained their underlying mechanisms in ways that are accessible without being reductive, and how I drew on LEAP resources to anchor the content in contexts that feel directly relevant to faculty practice.
