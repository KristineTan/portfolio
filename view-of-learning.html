My View of Learning

My work sits at the intersection of developmental psychology and learning science. I don't see learning as simply "consuming content" or "completing a course," but as a gradual reconstruction of how people perceive, interpret, and respond to the world.

I define learning as a relatively durable change in understanding, skill, or disposition — one that results from experience, reflection, and meaningful engagement with new information. This means learning is not a passive transfer of knowledge. It requires the learner to actively construct meaning, often by confronting the limits of what they already know.

This perspective informs my design in several ways:

  • I prioritize depth over breadth — fewer ideas explored more fully tend to produce more lasting understanding than comprehensive content coverage.
  • I design for transfer — helping learners connect new concepts to prior knowledge and real-world contexts, not just recall information on demand.
  • I treat confusion as productive — well-designed challenge and cognitive dissonance signal that real learning is happening.
  • I attend to motivation and affect — because engagement and emotional safety are preconditions for learning, not afterthoughts.
